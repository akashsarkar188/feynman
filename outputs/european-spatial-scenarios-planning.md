# Spatial Scenarios in European Spatial Planning: Literature Review

Date: 2026-04-28  
Slug: `european-spatial-scenarios-planning`

## Executive summary

**Scope note:** This review is based on a targeted, non-systematic set of retrievable sources (peer-reviewed case studies plus institutional guidance), not a full census of all European planning practice.

In the sources reviewed here, spatial scenarios are most often framed as **decision-support and deliberation tools** rather than pure forecasting tools [1][2][3][4][5].

Observation (source-backed): Governance-coupled scenario workflows (workshops, planning cycles, cross-level coordination) are repeatedly described as practically useful [3][4][5][6].  
Inference: Strong causal claims about implementation impact (e.g., “scenario X caused adoption Y”) remain under-evidenced in this corpus.

## 1) Working definition (review-specific)

For this review, “spatial scenario” means structured alternative futures with spatially explicit outputs (maps, allocations, indicator surfaces) used to compare planning options and support policy discussion [1][2][5].

## 3) Applicability in practice

### 3.1 Patterns with relatively stronger support

1. **Alternative appraisal and trade-off analysis**  
   In two case studies (Leipzig–Halle and Dublin), scenario workflows enabled explicit comparison of compact vs dispersed development under indicator frameworks [1][2].

2. **Policy stress-testing under uncertainty (guidance-level evidence)**  
   Institutional guidance sources recommend scenario portfolios (not single futures) to improve robustness of spatial planning choices [3][4][5]. Empirical adoption rates were not quantified in this review.

3. **Strategic dialogue and coordination support**  
   Institutional and participatory sources describe scenario processes as useful boundary objects for cross-level planning discussion [3][4][6]. Comparative effect sizes versus non-scenario approaches were not reported.

### 3.2 Limitations and caveats

1. **Limited external validity of case outcomes**  
   Compact/transit-oriented advantages were observed in two specific cases and are assumption-sensitive (time horizon, appraisal setup, local governance context) [1][2].

2. **Evidence asymmetry**  
   Retrieved sources report process quality and scenario outputs more often than downstream implementation tracking [3][4].

3. **Access constraints in this run**  
   Several potentially relevant publisher pages were blocked; related claims were removed or downgraded in this final version.

## 4) Tentative cross-source patterns (non-systematic)

1. **Scenarios as structured comparison devices** [1][3][4][5]  
   Confidence: **Medium** (mixed evidence types).

2. **Compact/transit-oriented options can outperform dispersed options in specific cases** [1][2]  
   Confidence: **Low-to-Medium** (two-case basis).

3. **Institutional guidance favors multi-scenario planning for robustness** [3][4][5]  
   Confidence: **Medium** (normative guidance, limited empirical uptake measures).

4. **Participatory/hybrid workflows may improve practical relevance but increase coordination complexity** [3][4][6]  
   Confidence: **Low-to-Medium**.

## 5) Disagreements and contested points

1. **Complexity vs usability:** richer models can reduce interpretability for non-technical stakeholders [3][5].
2. **Normative vs exploratory use:** scenarios can be used either for open exploration or for legitimizing preferred pathways [3][4].
3. **Institutional influence vs independent evaluation:** guidance documents are influential in practice but often not evaluated with causal policy-outcome designs [3][4][5].

## 6) Open questions

1. How often do scenario exercises measurably change adopted plans rather than only informing deliberation?
2. Which evaluation protocols fairly compare participatory-hybrid and model-centric workflows?
3. What minimum reproducibility standard (data, assumptions, code, governance metadata) should EU-funded scenario studies meet?
4. How should planners communicate deep uncertainty without disabling near-term action?
5. **MAJOR issue carried forward:** this review remains non-systematic and evidence-weighting across source types is not formalized.

## 7) Recommended next steps

1. **Cross-case replication:** apply one harmonized protocol across multiple EU regions and compare reproducibility + decision uptake.
2. **Implementation audit:** track scenario-informed plans over 3–5 years for enacted outcomes.
3. **Transparency benchmark:** score studies on open data, assumptions, uncertainty communication, and process documentation.
