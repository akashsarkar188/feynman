## (b) Extracted findings on practical applicability in Europe

1. **Scenarios are practically strongest when tied to explicit policy alternatives and measurable planning outcomes.** Leipzig-Halle and Dublin studies link scenario narratives to model outputs and evaluation indicators (environment/social/economic), enabling planning trade-off discussions rather than abstract foresight only [2][3].

2. **Compact/transit-oriented development repeatedly appears favorable versus dispersed growth in case-based analyses**, though results are time-horizon and cost-assumption sensitive (especially in large infrastructure appraisal) [2][3].

3. **Integrating formal planning intentions into land-change models is feasible but does not guarantee strong steering power.** Bucharest results suggest planning intentions can be weak drivers relative to other forces; some strategic-intention variants may worsen agricultural land outcomes [4].

4. **Participatory multi-scale scenario protocols improve contextual legitimacy and comparability across regions.** The 5-case European land-sharing/land-sparing framework shows a reusable structure for stakeholder-grounded, spatially explicit scenarios [5].

5. **For public policy institutions, scenario value is as much procedural as predictive.** PBL guidance explicitly frames uses for visioning, adaptive management, and transition governance, and warns against frequent operational misuse (e.g., reducing to one “most likely” scenario) [6].

6. **Institutional practice in the Netherlands indicates blended methods are now operational** (participation + GIS models + design visualization), with active uptake in workshops across ministries/provinces/municipalities [7].

7. **Inference:** Across sources, European applicability is highest where scenario work is embedded in governance processes (stakeholder workshops, cross-level planning, decision support) rather than treated as standalone model output [3][5][6][7].
