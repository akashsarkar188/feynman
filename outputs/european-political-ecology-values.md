# Values in European Political Ecology: Literature Review (2014–2026)

## Executive summary
In this **access-limited corpus review** (11 prioritized sources), values in European political ecology are mostly framed as political and institutional questions (justice, care, commoning, relationality, decoloniality), rather than as neutral preference aggregation [1,3,5,6,11].

What can be said with higher confidence from fully readable core texts is narrower:
- Justice-oriented critiques of growth-centered transition narratives are prominent in reviewed degrowth/EJ and EU bioeconomy work [1,3,5].
- Feminist and decolonial strands explicitly challenge dominant policy framings and valuation logics [5,6,11].
- Relational-values language is present in European nature-policy discussion, but this run did not establish strong longitudinal evidence of implementation effects [7,9].

What remains provisional in this run:
- Field-wide prevalence claims (e.g., "increasingly" across all European political ecology).
- Commons/new municipalism generalization beyond limited case evidence [10].

## Scope, method, and inclusion boundaries
- **Topic:** values in European political ecology.
- **Period:** 2014–2026 (plus one canonical pre-2020 anchor where needed).
- **Geography:** Europe-centered scholarship and EU policy-facing debates.

## Synthesis

### 1) Values as justice and political economy
Within the reviewed corpus, justice is repeatedly linked to material throughput, distribution of burdens, and conflict over development pathways [1,2]. In this sample, this is strongest where political ecology intersects with degrowth/environmental justice framing [1,3,5].

### 2) Care and feminist political ecology
Care is framed not only as a norm but as a principle for organizing transition priorities (social reproduction, maintenance, collective wellbeing) [5,11]. The available evidence here is largely conceptual/theoretical in this run.

### 3) Decolonial critiques of European transition policy
Decolonial political ecology contributions in the corpus challenge Eurocentric assumptions and highlight externalized harms in transition governance [3,5,6]. The direction of critique is clear in readable sources [3,5], while some broader field claims remain access-constrained.

### 4) Relational values in policy language
Relational values (attachment, reciprocity, responsibility) are explicitly discussed as policy-relevant in European nature-governance debates [7]. However, this run does **not** verify robust comparative evidence that relational framing consistently changes budgets/regulation/outcomes across jurisdictions [7,9].

## Methods landscape (observed)
In this corpus, methods are dominated by conceptual synthesis, discourse/policy analysis, and qualitative case-oriented work [1,3,5,6,9,10]. This review did not find strong longitudinal or quasi-experimental designs that isolate causal effects of value frameworks on policy outcomes.

## Consensus (bounded to this corpus)
1. In this source set, values are treated as contested socio-political commitments, not merely technical inputs [1,3,5,6].
2. Justice framing remains central and is often articulated with care/decolonial/relational critiques [3,5,7,11].
3. Growth-centric sustainability narratives are a recurrent target of critique, especially in bioeconomy-transition discussions [1,3,5].

## Disagreements and fault lines
1. **Reform vs rupture:** whether EU institutions can internalize these value critiques incrementally or require deeper systemic change [3,5,6].
2. **Universal frameworks vs plural/decolonial normativity:** whether universal sustainability language enables coordination or suppresses epistemic plurality [5,6].
3. **Policy pragmatism vs movement radicalism:** tension between administratively feasible instruments and anti-extractivist demands [1,3,5].

## Provisional / single-source-sensitive findings
- **Commons/new municipalism generalization:** current evidence here is narrow in this run and should be treated as provisional pending broader case coverage [10].
- **EU human–nature divide claims tied to single recent source:** keep tentative until cross-checked with additional independent analyses [8].

## Open questions
1. Which institutional designs best preserve value pluralism without reducing it to market valuation?
2. Under what conditions does relational-value language produce measurable policy-output changes?
3. How should European policy evaluation internalize externalized socioecological impacts along global supply chains?
4. What comparative evidence exists for scaling care/commoning institutions across distinct European governance contexts?

## Recommended follow-up work
- Build a coded review dataset: `paper × value frame × method × domain × evidence strength × access status`.
- Run a targeted full-text expansion (especially paywalled items) before making field-wide trend claims.
- Add comparative policy-outcome tracing (bioeconomy vs biodiversity governance).
