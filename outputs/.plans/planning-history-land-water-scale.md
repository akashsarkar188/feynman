# Plan: Scale, Planning History, and Land–Water Transformations in Europe

## Scope
Review how **scale** is conceptualized and operationalized from a **planning history** perspective in the European context, with emphasis on land–water interactions, ports, coasts, rivers, deltas, and transnational planning histories. Prioritize literature from 2015 onward, while retaining a small number of foundational sources where they are needed to define the field or provide historical anchoring.

## Key questions
1. How is scale defined or implied in planning history and historical spatial planning literature?
2. How have planners historically worked across scales in European land–water territories?
3. How do historical studies describe multi-scalar or cross-scalar planning in coasts, deltas, estuaries, port regions, and river basins?
4. How have local, regional, national, and transnational governance arrangements changed over time in these territories?
5. What historical roles did infrastructure, reclamation, dredging, flood protection, and urban expansion play in spatial transformation across scales?
6. What methods do planning historians use to study scale and spatial transformation?
7. What can historical cases teach about present-day scale conflicts, coordination problems, and territorial planning?

## Source types to search
- Peer-reviewed planning history, urban history, and spatial planning journals
- Port-city, delta, coastal, and water-management studies with historical or historiographical emphasis
- European research reports and edited volumes on spatial planning history
- Foundational theory on rescaling, European spatial planning, and transnational planning history

## Time period
- Default: **2015 onward**
- Foundational exceptions: older sources only when needed to define historical institutionalism, transnational planning history, or land–water planning relations
