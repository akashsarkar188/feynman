# Plan: Spatial Scenarios Applicability in Practice

- **Slug:** `spatial-scenarios-practice`
- **Date:** 2026-04-28
- **Objective:** Produce an evidence-backed literature review on what “spatial scenarios” are, where they are applied in practice, what methods dominate, and what limits real-world adoption.

## 1) Scope and key questions

### Core questions
1. How is *spatial scenario* defined across domains (urban planning, climate adaptation, land-use change, transport, ecosystem management)?
2. What methodological families are used (qualitative participatory scenarios, GIS-based simulation, cellular automata, ABM, integrated assessment)?
3. What evidence exists for practical applicability (policy uptake, planning workflows, decision support use, implementation outcomes)?
4. What barriers recur (data requirements, uncertainty communication, governance fit, stakeholder trust, reproducibility)?
5. Which evaluation criteria are used to judge scenario usefulness in practice (credibility, salience, legitimacy, usability)?

### Boundaries
- Focus on peer-reviewed and high-quality review/meta papers first, then representative applied case studies.
- Time window: primarily 2010–2026, with foundational earlier papers when repeatedly cited.
- Include cross-domain comparison to avoid domain-specific overgeneralization.

## 2) Source strategy

### Paper sources
- alphaXiv/arXiv discovery for scenario methodology and geospatial simulation.
- Broader web search for non-arXiv journal/review papers and authoritative summaries.

### Web/gray sources
- Official guidance pages from planning/environment institutions (e.g., IPCC scenario guidance, EU/UN/national planning bodies) when used to evidence “practice adoption”.

### Repositories/code
- Inspect selective open-source tools/frameworks linked to influential methods (if needed to assess practical reproducibility).

## 3) Expected output sections

1. Executive summary
2. Definitions and taxonomy of spatial scenarios
3. Methods landscape
4. Evidence of practical applicability (by sector)
5. Consensus findings
6. Disagreements and contested claims
7. Open questions and research gaps
8. Recommended next experiments / follow-up reading
9. Sources
