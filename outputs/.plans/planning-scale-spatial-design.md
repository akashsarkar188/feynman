# Plan: Scale in Spatial Design and Planning in the European Context

## Scope
Review how **scale** is defined, operationalized, and debated in spatial planning and spatial design literature focused on Europe. Prioritize 2015 onward, while retaining a few foundational sources where needed to define the concept or its planning relevance.

## Key questions
1. How is scale conceptualized in spatial planning, spatial design, and related urban/territorial governance literature?
2. How does scale matter in planning theory and practice?
3. How do multi-scalar and cross-scalar approaches appear in European planning?
4. How are local, regional, national, and transnational scales coordinated or contested?
5. How do scale issues appear in climate adaptation, infrastructure, metropolitan regions, landscapes, deltas, and ports?
6. What methods and frameworks are used to work across scales?
7. How is scale operationalized in planning practice, and what limits recur?

## Source types to search
- Peer-reviewed planning, urban studies, geography, and territorial governance articles
- European research reports and institutional syntheses
- Foundational conceptual papers on scale and rescaling in planning and state spatial restructuring
- Case studies on metropolitan governance, landscape planning, port-city relations, adaptation planning, and urban sprawl

## Time period
- Default: **2015 onward**
- Foundational exceptions: older sources only when they provide a key conceptual basis for scale, rescaling, or European spatial planning

## Expected sections in the final review
1. Executive summary
2. Scope and method
3. Conceptual map of scale in planning/design
4. Source matrix with full references, summaries, key arguments, scale usage, relevance, and methodology
5. Main debates and recurring authors
6. Key theories/frameworks
7. Gaps and emerging trends
8. Implications for a theoretical framework
9. Sources
