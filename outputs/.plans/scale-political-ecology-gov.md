# Plan: scale-political-ecology-gov

## Scope
Literature review on the concept of scale from a political ecology perspective in the European context, focusing on governance, land-water interactions, and stakeholder relations.

## Key questions
1. How is scale defined and conceptualized in political ecology and adjacent environmental governance literature?
2. How do scalar politics shape environmental governance, stakeholder relations, and decision-making across levels?
3. How are scale conflicts and rescaling processes studied in land-water systems: rivers, deltas, coasts, wetlands, watersheds, and resource territories?
4. How do climate adaptation, infrastructure, and territorial transformation rely on cross-scalar governance?
5. What methods and frameworks are used to operationalize scale in political ecology and governance studies?
6. What are the major debates, recurring authors, gaps, and emerging trends, especially in Europe-focused work?

## Source types to search
- Peer-reviewed political ecology, geography, and environmental governance journals
- European planning / water / coastal / delta governance literature
- Major European research reports and policy research outputs where scale is central
- Foundational pre-2015 works only when essential for definitions and theoretical framing

## Search strategy
- Search for scale, scalar politics, rescaling, multilevel governance, hydrosocial, river basin, delta, coast, territory, and stakeholder terms
- Prioritize European cases and Europe-based governance systems
- Cross-check metadata and source URLs for each accepted source

## Expected structure of final review
1. Scope and approach
2. Definitions and conceptualizations of scale in political ecology
3. Scalar politics and environmental governance
4. Land-water and stakeholder scale relations
5. Debates, recurring authors, and frameworks
6. Methods and operationalization
7. Gaps and emerging trends
8. Implications for a theoretical framework
9. Sources
