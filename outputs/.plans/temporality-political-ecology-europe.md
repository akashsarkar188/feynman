# Plan: temporality-political-ecology-europe

## Scope
Literature review on temporality from a political ecology perspective in the European context, focusing on planning, governance, climate, and socio-environmental transformations.

## Key questions
1. How has temporality been theorized in political ecology since 2015, and what foundational works remain necessary?
2. How are climate governance and environmental change temporalized (urgency, crisis, transition, adaptation, anticipation, delay)?
3. What do studies say about slow violence, temporal justice, and uneven socio-environmental futures?
4. How do infrastructure, landscapes, and resource governance produce multiple and cross-scalar temporalities?
5. How is temporality operationalized methodologically in political ecology and related planning/governance studies?
6. What are the main debates, recurring authors, gaps, and emerging trends in Europe-focused literature?

## Source types to search
- Peer-reviewed journal articles and edited volumes (2015+; include foundational pre-2015 works where essential)
- Major European research reports and policy-adjacent research outputs (e.g., EU Horizon, ESPON, JRC, ETC/EEA, national research institutes)
- Primary sources and, where necessary, the public repositories or project pages of key reports

## Search strategy
- Academic search via alphaXiv for political ecology + temporality + Europe + planning/governance/climate/infrastructure/resource governance
- Web search for recent European reports and project outputs
- Cross-check exact bibliographic details, DOI/URLs, and the presence of source texts

## Expected structure of final review
1. Scope and approach
2. Definitions and conceptualizations of temporality in political ecology
3. Major thematic findings
4. Debates and recurring authors
5. Methods and operationalization
6. Gaps and emerging trends
7. Framework implications for planning/governance
8. Sources
