# Plan: planning-history-temporality

## Scope
Literature review on definitions and usages of temporality from a planning history perspective in the European context.

## Key questions
1. How is temporality defined or implied in planning history, historical planning studies, and related urban history literature?
2. How do historians and planning scholars use temporality to analyze spatial planning, urban development, governance, and territorial transformation?
3. What roles do historical time, planning time, governance time, continuity/rupture, transition, and path dependency play in European cases?
4. How are climate, infrastructure, landscapes, and cross-border or multi-scalar transformations historicized through temporality?
5. What methods and historiographical approaches are used to study temporality in planning history?
6. What are the main debates, recurring authors, gaps, and emerging trends relevant to a theoretical framework?

## Source types to search
- Peer-reviewed planning history, urban history, geography, and planning journals
- Edited volumes/chapters on planning history and temporalities
- Major European research reports where they directly discuss long-term planning, historical governance, or temporal planning transitions
- Foundational pre-2015 works only when clearly necessary to define the field

## Search strategy
- Search for planning history + temporality/time/temporality of planning/long-term/short-term/history/path dependency/transition
- Prioritize European cases, European journals, and comparative studies in Europe
- Cross-check bibliographic metadata and URLs from publisher pages, repositories, or official reports

## Expected structure of final review
1. Scope and method
2. Definitions and conceptualizations of temporality in planning history
3. Major thematic findings
4. Debates and recurring authors
5. Methods and historiography
6. Gaps and emerging trends
7. Implications for a theoretical framework
8. Sources
