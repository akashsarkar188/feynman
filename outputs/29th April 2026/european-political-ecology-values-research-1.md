## Thematic clusters of values

### 1) Justice (distributional/procedural/recognitional; socioecological justice)
- Degrowth and EJ literatures are treated as potentially complementary political projects, with conflict-based grounding in material metabolism and unequal burdens/benefits [1][2].
- EU bioeconomy critiques emphasize that justice framing often remains intra-European while externalized harms persist (inference synthesized from [3][5]).

### 2) Care (feminist political ecology; social reproduction)
- Feminist political ecology interventions place care and social reproduction at the center of socioecological transformation and degrowth strategy [5][11].
- Inference: care operates both as ethical orientation and institutional design principle (commons/public service reorganization) [10][11].

### 3) Commons/commoning
- Commons-oriented municipal governance cases (Naples/Barcelona) indicate value shifts from managerial public administration toward co-governed common goods [10].
- **Uncertain detail:** full argument architecture not directly read due fetch failure; claim limited to metadata/snippet-level evidence [10].

### 4) Degrowth
- Degrowth is repeatedly positioned as critique of green-growth decoupling narratives and as a value-political project centered on sufficiency and justice [1][3][4][5].

### 5) Relational values
- Relational values (care, attachment, social bonding, meaning) are explicitly proposed as policy leverage points in Europe [7].
- Inference: relational value framing partially bridges political ecology and biodiversity governance debates by moving beyond strictly instrumental valuation [5][7][9].

### 6) Decolonial values
- Decolonial political ecology foregrounds coloniality of knowledge/power and challenges anthropocentric, extractivist policy ontologies [3][5][6][8].

## Methods used (dominant patterns)
- **Conceptual/theoretical synthesis:** strong in decolonial and degrowth-political ecology contributions [3][4][5][6].
- **Discourse/policy analysis:** EU strategy and governance texts; national policy discourse mapping [8][9].
- **Movement-oriented political economy / socio-metabolic analysis:** linking conflicts and material throughput [1][2].
- **Case-comparative institutional analysis:** commons/new municipalism cases [10].

## Tensions / disagreements in the literature
1. **Green growth vs degrowth:** disagreement over feasibility and justice implications of decoupling-led transition narratives [3][4][5].
2. **Instrumental vs relational/intrinsic value framing:** policy design tension between monetized ecosystem services and plural/relational ethics [5][7][9].
3. **Europe-centered justice vs planetary/decolonial justice:** dispute over whether EU policy justice claims adequately include outsourced extraction and colonial continuities [3][5][8].
4. **Institutional reform vs systemic transformation:** some works prioritize policy recalibration, others advocate deeper ontological/political-economic rupture (inference from contrasts across [3][5][6][10]).
