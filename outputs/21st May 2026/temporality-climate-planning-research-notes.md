# Research notes: Temporality, climate, and spatial planning/design in Europe

## Core sources and working notes

### 1) Wunderlich, Filipa Matos. 2023. *Time and temporality in urban design*. In *Temporal Urban Design*.
URL: https://doi.org/10.4324/9781315600581-3
- **Summary:** Conceptual chapter that frames urban design through time, rhythm, and place, useful for theorizing temporality in spatial design beyond climate adaptation alone.
- **Key concepts:** rhythms, temporality, atmosphere, place-time relations, design of urban experience.
- **Relevance:** provides a design-side conceptual anchor for temporal urbanism.
- **Methodology:** theoretical / conceptual chapter.

### 2) Monstadt, Jochen. 2022. *Urban and Infrastructural Rhythms and the Politics of Temporal Alignment*. Journal of Urban Technology 29(1): 69–77.
URL: https://doi.org/10.1080/10630732.2021.2007205
- **Summary:** Argues that infrastructures shape and order urban temporalities and that temporal alignment is a political process.
- **Key concepts:** infrastructural rhythms, temporal alignment/realignment, power, infrastructure ordering time.
- **Relevance:** central for infrastructure temporality, lock-in, and mismatch.
- **Methodology:** conceptual/theoretical article.

### 3) Egerer, Monika et al. 2021. *Urban change as an untapped opportunity for climate adaptation*. npj Urban Sustainability.
URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s42949-021-00024-y
- **Summary:** Shows that urban change itself can open windows for adaptation, rather than adaptation only responding to hazards.
- **Key concepts:** urban change, SETS systems, windows of opportunity, adaptation through change.
- **Relevance:** links temporality to incremental change, opportunity windows, and climate adaptation timing.
- **Methodology:** conceptual synthesis grounded in literature and urban SETS framing.

### 4) Krishnan, Supriya; Aydin, Nazli Yonca; Comes, Tina. 2024. *TIMEWISE: Temporal Dynamics for Urban Resilience - theoretical insights and empirical reflections from Amsterdam and Mumbai*. npj Urban Sustainability.
URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s42949-024-00140-5
- **Summary:** Develops a framework of endogenous and exogenous temporal dynamics for resilient planning and tests it in Amsterdam and Mumbai.
- **Key concepts:** timescapes, endogenous lifecycles, exogenous drivers, temporal flexibility, planning timeframes.
- **Relevance:** one of the strongest direct contributions to temporality in climate-resilient planning.
- **Methodology:** conceptual framework + multi-case qualitative analysis (interviews and document analysis).

### 5) Cerrada Morato, Lucía; Zimnicka, Agnieszka; Wilson, Judi. 2025. *Temporalities for, of, and in Planning: Exploring Post-Growth, Participation, and Devolution Across European Planning Reforms*. Urban Planning.
URL: https://doi.org/10.17645/up.9121
- **Summary:** Examines how time is mobilised in planning reforms across three European countries and argues temporalities in planning are relational.
- **Key concepts:** planning temporalities, speed-up, relational time, consensus, depoliticisation, post-growth.
- **Relevance:** strong for governance temporalities and time as a political instrument.
- **Methodology:** comparative qualitative analysis of national planning reforms.

### 6) Marušić, Damjan; Goličnik Marušić, Barbara. 2026. *Planning in Time: Temporal Resilience and the Governance of Urban Land Use Change*. Land.
URL: https://doi.org/10.3390/land15050780
- **Summary:** Reframes land-use planning as governance of change over time, using temporal resilience as the key lens.
- **Key concepts:** temporal resilience, land-use change, duration, adaptability, time quality assessment.
- **Relevance:** valuable for a theoretical framework on planning as temporal governance.
- **Methodology:** conceptual synthesis; no empirical case study.

### 7) García-Blanco, Gemma et al. 2025. *Dilemmas in Statutory Urban Planning When Addressing the Climate Adaptation Implementation Gap: Insights from Six European Cities*. Land.
URL: https://doi.org/10.3390/land14122304
- **Summary:** Tests a BRIDGE framework across six European cities to assess whether statutory planning can enable adaptation implementation.
- **Key concepts:** BRIDGE, agility, robustness, legal certainty, uncertainty, flexibility, implementation gap.
- **Relevance:** directly addresses temporal rigidity in planning instruments and the challenge of acting under uncertainty.
- **Methodology:** indicator-based screening tool applied to six-city pilot test.

### 8) Reckien, Diana et al. 2023. *Quality of urban climate adaptation plans over time*. npj Urban Sustainability.
URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s42949-023-00085-1
- **Summary:** Tracks quality in 327 European city adaptation plans from 2005–2020 and finds gradual improvement, but weak participation and monitoring.
- **Key concepts:** plan quality, ADAQA, learning over time, consistency, monitoring and evaluation.
- **Relevance:** important evidence on temporal improvement, but also the persistence of weak implementation-related temporalities.
- **Methodology:** large-N comparative plan-quality assessment.

### 9) Reckien, Diana et al. 2019. *Dedicated versus mainstreaming approaches in local climate plans in Europe*. Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews.
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rser.2019.05.014
- **Summary:** Compares dedicated climate plans and mainstreamed approaches, showing differences in integration and implementation pathways.
- **Key concepts:** mainstreaming, dedicated plans, integration, implementation success.
- **Relevance:** useful for short-term vs long-term planning logics and institutional embedding.
- **Methodology:** comparative review of local climate plans.

### 10) Grafakos, S. et al. 2020. *Integration of mitigation and adaptation in urban climate change action plans in Europe: A systematic assessment*. Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews.
URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rser.2019.109623
- **Summary:** Systematically assesses whether European urban plans integrate mitigation and adaptation and how strong that integration is.
- **Key concepts:** co-benefits, integration, plan coherence, trade-offs, mainstreaming.
- **Relevance:** links climate transitions to temporal coordination across policy domains.
- **Methodology:** systematic assessment of urban climate action plans.

### 11) Russel, Duncan et al. 2020. *Policy Coordination for National Climate Change Adaptation in Europe: All Process, but Little Power*. Sustainability.
URL: https://doi.org/10.3390/su12135393
- **Summary:** Finds that coordination processes exist in several EU states, but they often lack power to alter entrenched hierarchies.
- **Key concepts:** coordination, multi-level governance, long-term challenge, institutional hierarchy.
- **Relevance:** strong for governance temporalities and the gap between process and power.
- **Methodology:** comparative policy analysis across six EU member states.

### 12) Nowak, Maciej J. et al. 2024. *Comparison of Urban Climate Change Adaptation Plans in Selected European Cities from a Legal and Spatial Perspective*. Sustainability.
URL: https://doi.org/10.3390/su16156327
- **Summary:** Compares legal and spatial features of city adaptation plans in Greece, Spain, and Poland.
- **Key concepts:** legal-spatial integration, strategic planning, consistency across scales, implementation.
- **Relevance:** helpful for mismatches between supra-local diagnosis and municipal planning.
- **Methodology:** comparative legal-spatial case study.

### 13) van der Berg, Angela. 2023. *Climate Adaptation Planning for Resilient and Sustainable Cities: Perspectives from the City of Rotterdam (Netherlands) and the City of Antwerp (Belgium)*. European Journal of Risk Regulation.
URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-journal-of-risk-regulation/article/climate-adaptation-planning-for-resilient-and-sustainable-cities-perspectives-from-the-city-of-rotterdam-netherlands-and-the-city-of-antwerp-belgium/E697D3660B0344EC60E5716667631F4E
- **Summary:** Shows how Rotterdam and Antwerp integrate climate adaptation into planning at different depths; long-term sustainable development depends on handling synergies and trade-offs.
- **Key concepts:** integration, delta-city planning, co-benefits, trade-offs, monitoring.
- **Relevance:** strong for European delta governance and time-sensitive integration challenges.
- **Methodology:** comparative legal-policy analysis.

### 14) Dijstelbloem, Huub; Toom, Victor; de Vries, Annick. 2024. *Tides of Time: The Dutch Delta Works as Time-Mediating Climate Adaptation Infrastructure*. Tecnoscienza – Italian Journal of Science & Technology Studies 15(2): 17–37.
URL: https://doi.org/10.6092/issn.2038-3460/18434
- **Summary:** Interprets the Dutch Delta Works as infrastructure that mediates between temporal regimes and generates temporal compromises.
- **Key concepts:** infrastructure, time, kairos/krisis, temporal regimes, compromise.
- **Relevance:** one of the clearest sources on infrastructure temporality and long-horizon climate adaptation.
- **Methodology:** conceptual-historical analysis of the Delta Works.

### 15) Paauw, Mandy et al. 2022. *Adaptive Governance of River Deltas Under Accelerating Environmental Change*. Utrecht Law Review 18(2): 30–50.
URL: https://doi.org/10.36633/ulr.803
- **Summary:** Compares the Dutch Delta Programme and Mekong Delta Plan against adaptive governance principles.
- **Key concepts:** adaptive governance, legal sunsets, reflexive law, participation, boundaries, flexibility.
- **Relevance:** strong for governance temporalities, planned review cycles, and adaptive institutions.
- **Methodology:** content analysis / comparative document analysis.

### 16) Sayers, Paul et al. 2022. *A Framework for Cloud to Coast Adaptation: Maturity and Experiences from across the North Sea*. Land 11(6): 950.
URL: https://doi.org/10.3390/land11060950
- **Summary:** Develops a whole-system, long-term flood-risk adaptation framework spanning cloud-to-coast pathways across North Sea cases.
- **Key concepts:** whole-system thinking, flood adaptation pathways, maturity, long-term perspective.
- **Relevance:** useful for cross-scalar temporal planning in flood-prone coastal regions.
- **Methodology:** framework development with multiple case studies.

### 17) Maragno, Denis et al. 2020. *Land–Sea Interaction: Integrating Climate Adaptation Planning and Maritime Spatial Planning in the North Adriatic Basin*. Sustainability 12(13): 5319.
URL: https://doi.org/10.3390/su12135319
- **Summary:** Proposes a planning approach that combines climate adaptation planning and maritime spatial planning in a land-sea interaction context.
- **Key concepts:** MSP, CAP, land-sea interactions, integrated coastal governance.
- **Relevance:** helps connect spatial planning across terrestrial and marine time horizons.
- **Methodology:** methodological guideline plus North Adriatic case application.

### 18) Bisaro, Alexander et al. 2024. *Sea Level Rise in Europe: Governance context and challenges*. State of the Planet.
URL: https://sp.copernicus.org/articles/3-slre1/7/2024/
- **Summary:** Reviews progress in coastal adaptation governance across European sea basins and stresses transboundary coordination needs.
- **Key concepts:** sea-level rise governance, European sea basins, policy contexts, cross-border challenges.
- **Relevance:** important for multi-scalar and cross-border governance temporalities.
- **Methodology:** comparative governance review across sea basins.

### 19) Vila-Lage, Roberto et al. 2025. *Developing Cross-Border Spatial Planning: Establishing a Common Understanding Through a Forthcoming European Grouping of Territorial Cooperation Between Galicia and Portugal*. Land 14(3): 542.
URL: https://doi.org/10.3390/land14030542
- **Summary:** Examines cross-border spatial planning in the Galicia–Northern Portugal region and the formation of a common understanding through EGTC structures.
- **Key concepts:** cross-border planning, common understanding, EGTC, functional region, transboundary governance.
- **Relevance:** supports the multi-scalar / cross-border portion of the review.
- **Methodology:** comparative regional planning case study.

### 20) European Environment Agency. 2024. *Urban adaptation in Europe: what works?* EEA Report 14/2023.
URL: https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/analysis/publications/urban-adaptation-in-europe-what-works
- **Summary:** Major European report synthesising adaptation measures and enabling factors for implementation in cities.
- **Key concepts:** enabling conditions, implementation, political support, financing, monitoring.
- **Relevance:** major report on urban adaptation practice and implementation constraints across Europe.
- **Methodology:** report synthesis drawing on EEA publications, survey data, city interviews, and plan databases.

### 21) European Scientific Advisory Board on Climate Change. 2026. *Strengthening resilience to climate change – Recommendations for an effective EU adaptation policy framework*.
URL: https://climate-advisory-board.europa.eu/reports-and-publications/strengthening-resilience-to-climate-change-recommendations-for-an-effective-eu-adaptation-policy-framework

- **Relevance:** older but foundational for connecting adaptation policy to spatial planning.

## Working synthesis
- The literature clusters around four temporal problems:
  1. **Design/planning time**: urban design and planning systems are often static while climate change is dynamic.
  2. **Implementation time**: plans improve over time, but monitoring, evaluation, participation, and coordination remain weak.
  3. **Infrastructure time**: long-lived infrastructure and coastal/delta systems have lifecycles that exceed political cycles.
  4. **Governance time**: adaptation requires iterative review, legal sunsets, and multi-level coordination, but existing institutions often default to short-term, siloed decisions.
- Repeated terms across sources: rhythms, timescapes, temporal resilience, windows of opportunity, lock-in, path dependence, adaptive pathways, legal sunsets, integration/mainstreaming, long-term direction, monitoring and evaluation, cross-border coordination.
- Recurring authors/groups: Reckien / Olazabal / Simoes / Pietrapertosa / Grafakos; Monstadt; Wunderlich; Huitema / Schipper / Sayers; national and EU adaptation governance authors including Russel, Castellari, Dessai, Hildén, Jensen.
