## Summary
This document presents a literature review exploring the historical evolution of dominant values associated with land-water interaction in Europe. It identifies four key phases of value shifts, from early multi-functional local appreciations to state-led control, productivity maximization, and finally, integration of ecological and holistic considerations. The review also discusses the roles of key actors, scientific and technological advancements, and regional variations in shaping these transformations. It concludes by outlining consensus findings, inferred historiographic debates, open questions, and provides a list of sources with detailed access caveats.

## Strengths
- **[S1] Clear Structure and Periodization:** The review provides a well-defined historical periodization of value shifts, which helps in understanding the complex evolution of land-water interaction. The use of a `mermaid` graph further clarifies this trajectory.
- **[S2] Comprehensive Scope:** Beyond just value shifts, the review thoughtfully integrates discussions on key actors, the influence of science and technology, regional variations, and the emergence of ecological values, providing a holistic perspective.
- **[S3] Transparent Source Accessibility:** The "Evidence caveats" section and the "Verification Note" are explicit about the accessibility limitations of some cited sources (abstract/metadata only). This transparency is commendable for a review and allows readers to assess the confidence level of claims.
- **[S4] Identified Research Gaps:** The "Open questions and research gaps" section is well-articulated, pointing to avenues for future research and demonstrating a critical engagement with the existing literature.

## Weaknesses
- **[W1] MAJOR: Over-reliance on Limited-Access Sources for Critical Claims:** Several crucial claims, especially in the consensus findings and the description of phases, rely on sources primarily accessed via abstract or metadata only ([1], [5], [7], [8]). This significantly weakens the empirical rigor, as the full context and detailed arguments of these papers could not be thoroughly assessed. For instance, the very definition of "multi-functional values" and "utilitarianism" might be nuanced in the full texts of [5], [7], or [8].
- **[W2] MAJOR: Insufficient Detail on "Search strategy and inclusion criteria":** While the criteria are listed, the "Base corpus: Generated by `researcher` subagent output (`outputs/europe-land-water-values-research-1.md`)" is too vague. Without access to the `researcher` subagent's methods or the content of `europe-land-water-values-research-1.md`, the reproducibility of the search is severely hampered. It's unclear how the "selected English-language corpus" was formed beyond general criteria.
- **[W3] MINOR: Vague Link between "Verification Note" and specific claims:** The "Verification Note" states "All explicit factual claims in the main body have been anchored with inline citations..." and "...claims based on limited access to sources are explicitly noted as such." While the caveats section helps, the note itself doesn't explicitly state *which* specific claims within the main body are affected by limited access, requiring the reader to cross-reference every citation.
- **[W4] MINOR: Ambiguity in "Time frame: Early modern period (c. 16th century) to present (2026)":** Stating "2026" as the present is unusual for a literature review unless it implies a forward-looking analysis or includes very recent, pre-print literature. This could be interpreted as a potential zombie artifact from an earlier draft or an oversight.

## Verdict

However, the reliance on partially accessible sources for significant claims (W1) and the lack of detail on the corpus generation (W2) are substantial weaknesses. While not necessarily fatal for a preliminary review, these would likely lead to major revisions or rejection in a peer-reviewed venue (e.g., a journal specializing in environmental history or water management).
